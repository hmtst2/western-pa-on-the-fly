Hatch Guide

Understanding aquatic insects is an important part of successful fly fishing.

Trout feed on a variety of insects throughout the year, and knowing which insects are active can help anglers select appropriate fly patterns.

A hatch generally refers to aquatic insects emerging from the water into their winged adult stage.

Hatch timing can vary depending on water temperature, weather conditions, stream location, and the time of year.

Common Aquatic Insects

Several groups of aquatic insects are especially important to trout anglers. These include mayflies, caddisflies, stoneflies, and midges.

Mayflies

Mayflies spend most of their lives underwater as nymphs before emerging into winged adults.

Mayflies are commonly imitated using nymph, emerger, dry fly, and spinner patterns.

Common examples include Blue-Winged Olives, Hendricksons, March Browns, and Light Cahills.

Caddisflies

Caddisflies begin their lives underwater as larvae. Some species build protective cases from sand, small stones, or plant material.

They eventually develop into pupae before emerging as winged adults.

Popular fly patterns include the Elk Hair Caddis and various caddis larva and pupa imitations.

Stoneflies

Stoneflies spend their immature stages underwater as nymphs.

Unlike many mayflies, stonefly nymphs usually crawl onto rocks or vegetation before emerging into winged adults.

Stonefly nymph patterns can be effective when fishing near the streambed.

Insect Life Stages

Aquatic insects pass through different developmental stages.

Some are available to trout as underwater larvae or nymphs, while others become vulnerable during emergence or as adults.

Observing insects around rocks, logs, and the water's surface can help anglers determine which fly patterns to use.

Seasonal Hatch Reference

Different aquatic insects become active throughout the fishing season.

The following table provides a general overview of common insect activity in Western Pennsylvania.

Actual hatch timing varies by stream, elevation, and weather conditions.

Season Common Insects Suggested Fly Patterns
Spring Quill Gordons, Hendricksons, Blue-Winged Olives Quill Gordon, Hendrickson, BWO patterns
Late Spring / Summer March Browns, Light Cahills, caddisflies March Brown, Light Cahill, Elk Hair Caddis
Fall Blue-Winged Olives, midges, some caddisflies BWO patterns, Zebra Midges, caddis imitations

Trout Beneath the Surface

While aquatic insects are an important food source for trout, understanding the environment where trout live is equally valuable.

Trout often use underwater structure, current changes, and deeper areas of streams for shelter and feeding.
